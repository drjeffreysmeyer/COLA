Activity 2.1.19. Explorations with The Mixture Parallelogram Tool.

In this activity, you will engage with some of the core mixture questions of this course. Build intuition now and revisit later. You will use the Mixture Parallelogram Tool to visualize, explore, and understand mixtures of vectors in \(\R^2\text{.}\)
🔗
A pre-made version of the Mixture Parallelogram Tool can be found at GeoGebra ID: qAC4TXwy.
  1. Finding serving sizes to create a given mixture. By playing with the sliders, find the serving sizes \(r\) and \(s\) so that \(r\begin{bmatrix}3\\1\end{bmatrix}+s\begin{bmatrix}-1\\2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}3\\8\end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Analyzing the set of all possible mixtures. Move the vectors \(\vect{v}\) and \(\vect{w}\text{,}\) and sliders \(r\) and \(s\text{,}\) to analyze the truth of each of the following claims. In complete sentences, and using your mixture parallelogram, justify why the claim seems true or seems false.
    🔗
    1. Every vector in the plane is a linear combination of \(\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\) and \(\begin{bmatrix}-1\\1\end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Every vector in the plane is a linear combination of \(\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\) and \(\begin{bmatrix}2\\2\end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
  3. Holding one slider constant. Slide \(r\) to \(1\) and keep it there for the moment. Look at the tip of your mixture vector. Right click on the point and select "trace" and then start sliding \(s\) around (while not changing \(r\)). What shape is created by the collection of tip points? Reset and try this again by fixing \(s=1\) and sliding \(r\text{.}\)
