Example 9.4.6.

Consider the matrix \(A=\left[ \begin{array}{rr}3 \amp 2\\-5 \amp 5 \end{array}\right].\) A direct computation shows
🔗
\begin{equation*} \disc(A)=8^2-4\cdot 25=-36\lt 0, \end{equation*}
hence \(A\) has 2 complex eigenvalues and we will look to find a rotationalization for \(A\text{.}\) It has characteristic polynomial
🔗
\begin{equation*} p_A(\lambda)=\lambda^2-8\lambda+25 \end{equation*}
and the quadratic formula gives its eigenvalues to be \(4\pm 3i\text{.}\) We focus on the eigenvalue \(\lambda=4+ 3i=5e^{\arccos(4/5)i}\text{.}\) Plugging \(\lambda\) into the matrix equation \((A-\lambda I)\vect{x}=\vect{0}\) and row reducing, we have
🔗
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{ll|r}-1-3i \amp 2 \amp 0\\ -5 \amp 1-3i \amp 0 \end{array}\right]\sim\left[ \begin{array}{lr|r}1+3i \amp -2 \amp 0\\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{array}\right] \end{equation*}
From which we get that \(y=\frac{(1+3i)}{2}x\text{,}\) and hence we get the complex eigenvector
🔗
\begin{equation*} \vect{v}=\left[ \begin{array}{ll}2\\1+3i \end{array}\right]=\left[ \begin{array}{rr}2\\1 \end{array}\right]+\left[ \begin{array}{rr}0\\3 \end{array}\right]i. \end{equation*}
Here \(\vect{u}:=\left[ \begin{array}{rr}2\\1 \end{array}\right]\) is the real part and \(\vect{w}:=\left[ \begin{array}{rr}0\\3 \end{array}\right]\) is the imaginary part of the eigenvector. They are linearly independent and \(\mathcal{B}:=\{\vect{u},\vect{w}\}\) is our rotation basis.
🔗
Direct computation using the strategy above gives \(A\vect{u} = 4\vect{u}-3 \vect{w}\) and \(A\vect{w} = 3\vect{u}+4 \vect{w}\text{.}\)
🔗
Symbolically we have the rotationalization
🔗
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{rr}3 \amp 2\\-5 \amp 5 \end{array}\right]=\left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp 0\\1 \amp 3 \end{array}\right]\left[ \begin{array}{rr}4 \amp 3\\-3 \amp 4 \end{array}\right]\left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp 0\\1 \amp 3 \end{array}\right]^{-1}. \end{equation*}
Verbally, \(A\) is a uniform stretch by a factor of \(r=5\) followed by a rotation in \(\mathcal{B}\)-coordinates by an angle of \(\theta =\arccos\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)\) radians moving \(\vect{u}\) away from \(\vect{w}\text{.}\)
🔗
