Activity 9.1.3. Finding and Analyzing Invariant Lines.

In this activity, you will use The Invariant Line Finder Tool to find and analyze invariant lines and their associated scaling factors. You will discover how different matrices have different numbers of invariant lines. In certain situations, you will find similar matrix decompositions.
🔗

Instructions.

An interactive GeoGebra window showing showing a line and transformed line. Move the line and observe how the transformed line changes. Is the line and transformed line the same? Find all invariant lines and their associated scaling factors.
🔗
🔗
Start with \(A=\left[ \begin{array}{rr}2.5 \amp -0.5 \\ -0.5 \amp 2.5 \end{array}\right]\text{.}\)
🔗
  1. Finding invariant lines and scaling factors.
    🔗
    Explore. Move your point \(B\) and find all invariant lines. (Pro Tip: Try moving \(B\) to grid points, where GeoGebra can lock in values.) For each invariant line, find the associated scaling factor. First, can you visually see the scaling factor? After approximating visually, you can compute the stretch factor exactly by analyzing the ratio of the length of \(T(B)\) to the length of \(B\text{.}\) Does the scaling factor include a reflection? If you can find two distinct invariant lines, find a similar matrix decomposition for \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Varying the matrix - Part 1.
    🔗
    Consider our geometric transformations of the plane: uniform stretches, stretches, reflections, shears, rotations, and projections. How many distinct lines are invariant? What is the most (resp. least) number of lines that can be invariant?
    🔗
    For example, analyze each of the following:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 2 \end{array}\right] \hspace{1pc} \left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 3 \end{array}\right] \hspace{1pc} \left[ \begin{array}{rr}-1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array}\right] \hspace{1pc} \left[ \begin{array}{rr}0 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{array}\right] \hspace{1pc} \left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 2 \end{array}\right] \hspace{1pc} \left[ \begin{array}{rr}3 \amp -4 \\ 4 \amp 3 \end{array}\right] \hspace{1pc} \end{equation*}
    🔗
  3. Varying the matrix - Part 2.
    🔗
    Repeat your above analysis for each of the following matrices (i.e. find invariant lines, find scaling factors, and find similar matrix decomposition):
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} M_1 = \left[ \begin{array}{rr}1.25 \amp 2.25 \\ -0.25 \amp 2.75 \end{array}\right], \hspace{0.0pc} M_2 = \left[ \begin{array}{rr}-0.4 \amp -1.2 \\ -1.2 \amp 1.4 \end{array}\right], \hspace{0.0pc} M_3 = \left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp -2.5 \\ 10 \amp 2 \end{array}\right] \end{equation*}
    Explain how knowing the different number of invariant lines a matrix has tells you something about the nature of the associated transformation (e.g. stretch vs. shear vs rotation etc).
    🔗
