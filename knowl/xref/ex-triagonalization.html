Example 9.4.4.

Consider the matrix \(A=\left[ \begin{array}{rr}1.5 \amp 1\\-0.25 \amp 2.5 \end{array}\right].\) A direct computation shows
🔗
\begin{equation*} \disc(A)=4^2-4\cdot 4=0, \end{equation*}
hence \(A\) has 1 repeated real eigenvalue, and since \(A\) is not scalar, we will look to find a triagonalization for \(A\text{.}\) It has characteristic polynomial
🔗
\begin{equation*} p_A(\lambda)=\lambda^2-4\lambda+4=(\lambda-2)^2, \end{equation*}
so its eigenvalue is \(\lambda=2\text{.}\) Plugging \(\lambda\) into the standard matrix equation and row reducing, we have
🔗
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{rr|r}-0.5 \amp 1 \amp 0\\ -0.25 \amp 0.5 \amp 0 \end{array}\right]\sim\left[ \begin{array}{rr|r}1 \amp -2 \amp 0\\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{array}\right] \end{equation*}
From which we get that \(x=2y\text{,}\) and hence we get the eigenvector
🔗
\begin{equation*} \vect{v}=\left[ \begin{array}{r}2\\1 \end{array}\right]. \end{equation*}
We now find a generalized eigenvector that we hold on to to shear after having performed our uniform stretch, i.e. a vector \(\vect{w}\) such that \(A\vect{w}=2\vect{w}+\vect{v}\text{.}\) In other words, we solve the following matrix equation
🔗
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{rr|r}-0.5 \amp 1 \amp 2\\ -0.25 \amp 0.5 \amp 1 \end{array}\right]\sim\left[ \begin{array}{rr|r}1 \amp -2 \amp -4\\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{array}\right] \end{equation*}
From which we get that \(x=-4+2y\text{,}\) and hence, when \(y=1\text{,}\) we get the generalized eigenvector
🔗
\begin{equation*} \vect{w}=\left[ \begin{array}{r}-2\\1 \end{array}\right]. \end{equation*}
The vectors \(\vect{v}\) and \(\vect{w}\) are linearly independent and \(\mathcal{B}:=\{\vect{v},\vect{w}\}\) is our generalized eigenbasis.
🔗
Symbolically we have the triagonalization
🔗
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{rr}1.5 \amp 1\\-0.25 \amp 2.5 \end{array}\right]=\left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp -2\\1 \amp 1 \end{array}\right]\left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp 1\\0 \amp 2 \end{array}\right]\left[ \begin{array}{rr}2 \amp -1\\1 \amp 1 \end{array}\right]^{-1}. \end{equation*}
Verbally, \(A\) is a uniform stretch by a factor of \(2\) followed by a shear in the \(\mathcal{B}\)-coordinates, taking the vector \(2\vect{w}\) 1 unit in the \(\vect{v}\) direction.
🔗
