Theorem 5.3.6. Fundamental Theorem of Bases.

If \(W\subset \vect{R}^m\) is a subspace, \(\mathcal{B}=\{\vect{v}_1,\ldots, \vect{v}_n\}\subset W\) is an ordered basis for \(W\text{,}\) and \(\vect{w}\in W\) is a vector, then there exists a unique collection of coefficients \(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n\in \vect{R}\) such that
\begin{equation*} \vect{w}=x_1\vect{v}_1+\cdots +x_n\vect{v}_n. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Since \(\mathcal{B}\) spans \(W\text{,}\) \(\vect{w}\in \spn(\mathcal{B})\text{.}\) Suppose now
\begin{equation*} \vect{w}=x_1\vect{v}_1+\cdots +x_n\vect{v}_n, \end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*} \vect{w}=y_1\vect{v}_1+\cdots +y_n\vect{v}_n, \end{equation*}
for some \(x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_n\in\vect{R}\text{.}\) Subtracting one from the other gives:
\begin{equation*} \vect{0}=(x_1-y_1)\vect{v}_1+\cdots +(x_n-y_n)\vect{v}_n. \end{equation*}
The independence of \(\mathcal{B}\) (Proposition 2.5.9) implies each coefficient \(x_i-y_i=0\text{,}\) or in other words, \(x_i=y_i\text{.}\) We deduce that the coefficients are unique.
